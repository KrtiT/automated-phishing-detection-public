## What comparable GWU work actually establishes

1. Matthew Grofsky (2025), *AegisShield: Democratizing Cyber Threat Modeling with
   Generative AI*, public author dissemination copy,
   https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16888310. Chapter 3, section 3.1, printed page 39
   (PDF page 56), explicitly describes iterative design and multiple development
   and evaluation cycles. This is a useful example for explaining artifact
   development as part of a coherent research process. It is not permission to
   misstate when this study's redesign was conceived.
2. Raj Shah (2025), *Enhancing Privacy and Accuracy in Facial Recognition with
   Synthetic Data Generated by Pre-Trained GANs*, public author dissemination copy,
   https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16995670. Section 4.3, printed pages 87–90
   (PDF pages 105–108), reports mixed hypothesis findings, including rejection of
   H1. Section 4.4 discloses a validation change. The presence of these statements
   is verifiable; the filename and repository listing are not independent evidence
   of degree acceptance. Do not copy its statistical choices: failure to detect a
   difference is not an equivalence test, and performance-informed fold changes
   should not be treated as fresh confirmatory validation.

These are two unique works, not four: concept and version DOI records duplicate
the same work. Both metadata records link ProQuest versions. Official library
guidance endpoints were inaccessible in this retrieval. No verified GWU rule
requiring a minimum number of supported hypotheses was found. The existing
manuscript's supplied GWU template remains the formatting basis; these examples
do not replace it or establish new institutional requirements.

## Contribution boundaries

The initial study already provides a concrete, auditable joint test of structural
features, character modeling, source/domain shift monitoring and real HTTP
behavior under explicit risk/resource limits. Its contribution is the connected
evidence and implementation, not an invention claim for Logistic-L1, transformers,
GMMs, confidence cascades or domain-disjoint splitting.

The existing research-basis record identifies close URL/cascade/shift prior work
(Ahamed, ExpertFusion, Alajaji, Rashid, Tsai, CascadeBERT and CADE). This focused
review does not establish priority over all of those works. Neither scheme
normalization nor connection reuse is itself a novel algorithm. A follow-up can
add value by isolating a source-representation dependency or a client-side
measurement bottleneck with controlled comparisons and precisely stating how it
changes an engineering decision. Such a claim requires the comparison results;
the design alone does not establish novelty or successful improvement.

## Narrative that the evidence can support

The account should proceed through the engineering questions: what the artifact
was designed to achieve; what each controlled evaluation established; why a
component success did not meet a joint requirement; which specific mechanism the
diagnosis identified; what was changed; and what the separately specified
comparison showed. Explain successful components and usable design knowledge
alongside adverse results. Avoid a limitations-only summary, but also avoid
retroactively changing the initial hypotheses or claiming an unmeasured repair.

The extension was requested after the initial outcomes. This is a normal factual
chronology to explain once in Methods and connect to Results/Discussion. There is
no need for apologetic "last-minute" language, and no basis for describing it as
planned before those outcomes.

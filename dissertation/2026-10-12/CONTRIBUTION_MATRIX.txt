Candidate A — When selective computation can change detection

Prior knowledge: ensemble and selective execution can trade accuracy for cost.
Specific question: does an expensive expert repair the cheap model's errors in
the regions that an inexpensive gate can actually identify under an FPR budget?
Measured evidence: the retained certified negatives show zero repairs and 218
introduced errors; 2,261 errors are shared. No selector of these unchanged binary
decisions can reach less than 90.5487% FPR on that cohort. Internally, repairs and
introduced errors coexist and occupy different score regions.
Completed development evidence: a twelve-band replay selected a symmetric .20
band with 34 net added internal detections and 40 fewer false positives at
1,146/34,593 logical calls. Its matched-budget hash control added five detections
and nine false positives. The selected directional band added 35 detections and
42 false positives. Both selected rules added eleven certified external errors
with no repair. A separate implementation verified all 119 aggregates and both
selections. These outcomes establish development behavior, not new-model timing
or independent confirmation. Completed R evaluation: 1,392 positive and 1,262
negative domains, one selected URL per domain, with unchanged models and
thresholds. Both selected gates introduce 18 false positives with no selected
repairs; all systems detect all positives. Two separate recall-only
noninferiority statements are supported, conditional on independent labeled
pairs. C measures the selected symmetric gate at 1.646 times structural
summed-scoring time with 17 expert calls per 1,000 requests. That is actual
computation, not an HTTP service comparison.
Design implication: examine error complementarity before optimizing an uncertainty
band; high aggregate expert recall or higher call volume does not imply useful
conditional improvement. The algebraic overlap bound is elementary; the research
value is its tested engineering application, not a claim of a new theorem.
Boundary: results concern these representations, operating points and populations.

Candidate B — Separate invariance, ranking and operating-point transfer

Prior knowledge: URL formatting and source composition can impair generalization.
Specific question: which representation change repairs a measured input
sensitivity, and what detection signal is lost at the same time?
Measured evidence: 43,294 exact saved-feature replays; external structural AUC
.8512 but at most 20/69 positives recovered at empirical FPR <=1%; comparison D
has exact scheme invariance but loses 184 detections while removing 251 false
positives. The two properties cannot be used interchangeably.
Completed F experiment: fixed weights, scaler and threshold; only the scheme
prefix changes. Forcing HTTPS removes 2,211 certified false positives but loses
47 of 69 gold detections. FPR remains 2.0024% and recall falls to 31.8841%.
This isolates input spelling from D's combined representation, refit and
calibration change. Neither result supports a scheme-only remedy.
Design implication: test low-FPR tail separation after making representation
stable; an invariant feature map is not a deployment-quality certificate.
Boundary: D used a different population and combined refitting/calibration; it
cannot alone isolate a single causal mechanism or support future-traffic claims.

Candidate C — Attribute service improvement to the work actually optimized

Prior knowledge: connection ownership and contention influence HTTP latency.
Specific question: how much end-to-end improvement survives when detector work,
request ordering and routing are held fixed, and where is the cost located?
Measured evidence: S's paired structural-workload result improves client-observed
tail latency while preserving comparable predictions; strict response equivalence
is not met. The selected workload does not establish transformer acceleration.
Selected scope: restrict S's claim to the measured synthetic structural,
closed-loop client/server workload, as the advisor explicitly permits. Do not
repeat the 80-arm comparison or claim transformer-service acceleration. Actual
cost of the revised router is a separate bounded comparison, not a replacement
for S. Independent run pairs, not individual requests, supply replication for
speedup uncertainty. The final manuscript/deck sweep must enforce this scope.
Design implication: report input sensitivity, model calls and client/server costs
separately so an HTTP improvement is not mistaken for better detection or faster
transformer computation. Connection reuse by itself is not the novelty claim.

Narrative direction

The dissertation asks when additional representation and computation deliver
useful detection at a constrained false-positive rate. It first measures the
structural model's benefit over length alone, then asks whether the transformer
repairs the remaining errors, and finally measures what serving the chosen
detector costs. The external findings expose a separation between representation
stability, score discrimination and low-FPR operation. The revised experiments
test that separation explicitly. The contribution should be stated through
measured design decisions a reader can reuse, not a claim that every component
or integration technique is new.

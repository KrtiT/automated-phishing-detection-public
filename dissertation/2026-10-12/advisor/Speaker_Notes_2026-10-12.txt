SLIDE 2 — Three questions, unchanged numerical commitments

Representation, monitoring and inline operation must be judged together.

RQ1: incremental recall with internal and external FPR ≤1%

RQ2: shift sensitivity ≥80%, reference alerts ≤5%, useful future routing

RQ3: recall loss ≤2 points, calls ≤30%, p95 ≤200 ms, errors <0.1%

Speaker notes: RQ1 requires both structural-over-length and cascade-over-structural gains. RQ2 additionally requires external policy FPR ≤1% and a positive paired false-negative reduction. RQ3 retains certified-negative and Tranco safeguards for both systems. Numerical requirements are study-defined, not universal literature cutoffs.

Evidence: §§1.4.1–1.4.3; corrected-primary-gates.csv

SLIDE 4 — Structural information adds recall; specificity does not transfer

RQ1 separates representation value from the chosen operating point.

Structural-over-length gain: +64.07 points internal; +76.81 external

Internal structural FPR: 0.7747%; external certified FPR: 90.5487%

Original gate selects no evaluation calls, so cascade recall gain is zero

Speaker notes: Internal interval [59.99,67.63] points is domain-clustered. Corrected external envelope [57.27,87.19] is conditional on independent labeled pairs. The transformer alone has 99.2791% external certified FPR. H1 remains unsupported because all conditions are required.

Evidence: §§3.8, 4.2; Tables 4.2–4.3



SLIDE 5 — Diagnose the shared errors before redesigning the gate

A selector cannot fix errors shared by both unchanged decisions.

Original certified negatives: 2,261 shared false positives / 2,497

Transformer repairs 0 and introduces 218 negative errors

Observed unchanged-decision selector floor: 90.5487% FPR

Speaker notes: This elementary overlap bound applies to the two fixed binary decisions and this cohort, not all possible recalibrations, representations or future populations. It directs effort toward discriminative information rather than more routing alone.

Evidence: §4.8.1; saved-score-routing-replay-v2.json



SLIDE 6 — Changing the scheme moves both classes

F isolates spelling sensitivity with model, scaler and threshold fixed.

Retained spelling: TP 69/69; FP 2,261/2,497

Forced HTTPS: TP 22/69; FP 50/2,497

2,211 fewer false positives, but 47 fewer positive detections

Speaker notes: F changes the prefix and preserves every character after the colon. HTTPS FPR remains 2.0024%, recall becomes 31.8841%. This is not a live-site transport experiment or an isolated causal effect of is_https. D separately achieves exact invariance on 8,622 pairs but loses 184 detections while removing 251 false positives after refit/recalibration.

Evidence: §4.10.1; Table 4.21; F-formatting.csv



SLIDE 7 — A wider gate must select repairs, not simply more cases

Development benefit did not transfer to R's retained domain-balanced frame.

R: 1,392 positive + 1,262 negative domains; one URL per domain

Symmetric gate: 21 calls / 2,654; 0 selected repairs; 18 new false positives

All systems detect 1,392 positives; structural FP 1,028, symmetric FP 1,046

Speaker notes: The width .20 rule was selected using bounded development replay before R scoring. The directional rule also creates 18 negative harms. Matched-count controls create two and one false positives. Across all records the transformer repairs one negative error but introduces 194; the selected bands miss that isolated repair. R is retrospective and does not establish campaign independence.

Evidence: §§3.16.1–3.16.2, 4.10.2; Table 4.22; R-routing.csv



SLIDE 8 — More precise recall evidence, without changing the original verdict

Agreement must be accompanied by a nondegenerate uncertainty interval.

Original 69 positive pairs: corrected 95% envelope ±6.1533 points

R's 1,392 pairs: 95% envelope ±0.3143 points

Two new recall-only noninferiority statements pass the −2-point margin

Speaker notes: Use the exact marginal-discordance envelope; Newcombe method 10 is a sensitivity check. The new statements concern original and symmetric cascades versus transformer on R, under independent labeled-pair assumptions. They do not establish specificity, joint deployment adequacy or original H3. Source-date grouping does not prove campaign independence.

Evidence: §§3.8, 4.10.2; Table 4.3; R-paired-contrasts.csv



SLIDE 9 — Rare expert calls appear in p99, not necessarily p95

C measures actual model work, separate from the HTTP service.

Symmetric: 17 expert calls / 1,000 requests; 1.646× structural time

Median run p95: 0.223 ms; p99: 6.769 ms

Always-transformer: 30.781× structural summed-scoring time

Speaker notes: Thirty arms are six paths by five repeats; 30,000 measured requests plus 3,000 excluded warmups. Zero recorded failures. The five cyclic rotations are not fully position-balanced. Ratios are medians of paired summed scoring times and quantiles are medians of run quantiles, not pooled HTTP latencies. No run-level confidence interval is claimed.

Evidence: §4.10.3; Table 4.23; C-cost-summary.csv and C-all-arms.csv



SLIDE 10 — A monitor signal is not automatically a useful action

RQ2 evaluates both distribution recognition and what happens afterward.

External window detection: 115/132 = 87.12%

Reference false alerts: 28/252 = 11.11%

Future routing adds 218 false positives and no gold detections

Speaker notes: The monitor exceeds the 80% sensitivity target but misses the 5% reference-alert target. Policy FPR is 2479/2497, gold recall remains 69/69, and no positive population improvement lower bound is established. Overlapping windows and future activations are dependent. H2 is unsupported, with an identified reason rather than an unexplained failure label.

Evidence: §§4.3, 5.1; Tables 4.4–4.5



SLIDE 11 — The client-path modification has a measured benefit

S changes client connection ownership while keeping structural scoring fixed.

Median paired p95 reduction: 79.68% at concurrency 64

Worker successful p95: 71.999 ms; 0 errors / 100,000 attempts

99,999 / 99,999 comparable predictions agree

Speaker notes: S uses synthetic structural-only closed-loop HTTP work. Ratio .203249, 97.5% CI [.18790,.21351]. Strict response agreement remains unmet because admission sequence differs and one shared-client request times out. All 80 final arms are retained; no predecessor arm is pooled. This does not establish transformer-service acceleration or production capacity.

Evidence: §4.9; Tables 4.18–4.20 and B.1



SLIDE 12 — Verify fitting inputs instead of trusting a plausible metric

P directly links each new shuffled-label fit to its consumed label vector.

Five seeds, 42–46; all converge below 5,000 iterations

Validation AUC range 0.3940–0.6965; AP 0.4027–0.7683

Every outcome retained; no seed selection or favorable reruns

Speaker notes: True validation labels select each descriptive threshold. Near-0.5 score centers do not imply chance ranking; AUC depends on order. The audit proves label linkage for the five new fits, not historical retroauthentication, absence of leakage or a formal .05 permutation test. Five permutations would not support such a test.

Evidence: §4.10.4; Table 4.24; P-all-seeds.csv



SLIDE 13 — What the dissertation adds to established methods

The contribution is tested engineering knowledge about constrained decisions.

Measure same-example repair before optimizing deferral

Test low-FPR discrimination alongside representation invariance

Separate model calls, actual computation and client-observed service cost

Speaker notes: Ahamed et al. already examine normalization and two-source transfer. ExpertFusion's repository documents calibrated expert fusion. This dissertation does not claim those components as inventions. Its empirical connection among error overlap, risk constraints, a fixed routing revision and measured computation provides a concrete design decision. The overlap bound itself is not presented as a new theorem.

Evidence: §§2.2–2.9, 5.2; Tables 2.1 and 5.1

SLIDE 15 — The technical answer is actionable

Change what the evidence identifies, and test every consequence.

Shared decision errors require changed information or operating-point development

A useful route must find repairs while controlling harms and compute

Optimize the measured service path without calling it better detection

Speaker notes: This connects diagnosis to modification without hiding tradeoffs. The current experiments reject the wider external gate, establish exact representation conformance with its cost, support bounded recall-only statements and demonstrate a client-path latency effect. New studies would need new populations and declared development rules, not relabeled historical outcomes.

Evidence: §§5.2–5.6
